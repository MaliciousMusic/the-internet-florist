Concurrently as the government of the Tokugawa shogunate started to allow local domains to issue their own 4 mon Kan'ei Tsūhō coins they themselves started minting the Bunkyū Eihō (文久永宝) cash coin from 1863, the inscription on the coin refers to the Bunkyū era (1861–1864), Eihō (永宝) could be translated as "eternally circulating treasure" indicating a very hopeful name for the new series which did not come into fruition as the Japanese mon was superseded by the yen during the Meiji restoration.
