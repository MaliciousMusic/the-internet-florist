# Established e-card / digital greeting card incumbents: pricing, metrics, features and trends (as of October 2026)

_Compiled 2026-10-02. "Seen 2026-10-02" means observed that day by fetching the page directly, reading the official App Store listing, or (where the official site returned HTTP 403 to automated fetches: jacquielawson.com, americangreetings.com, bluemountain.com, doozycards.com, groupgreeting.com) reading the search-engine index of the official page. Older data is flagged with its year._

## Q1. What do the main incumbents charge in 2026 (annual subscriptions, per-card prices, free tiers)?

### Takeaway
Consumer e-card subscriptions cluster at about **US$36/year or US$8/month**. That is the price of Jacquie Lawson, American Greetings and Blue Mountain, and all three now sit under the same owner (American Greetings / AG Interactive, controlled by Elliott since Feb 2025). Cheaper app-first players such as Greetings Island charge about $23.49/year. Per-card digital pricing is anchored by Moonpig eCards at £/$/€0.99. Group cards cost $5.99–$19.99 per card or board, plus B2B plans of $25–$399/month. Invitation platforms (Evite, Paperless Post, Punchbowl) charge per event ($17.99–$99.99), per guest ($0.50–$1.44) or $4–$16/month. Hallmark has had no e-card service since April 2021.

### Inferences
- Jacquie Lawson, American Greetings and Blue Mountain charge almost the same amount (≈$36/yr; $7.99–$8/mo), and the JL page offers a +$18 Blue Mountain bundle. Together this looks like coordinated portfolio pricing under one owner (see Q3) rather than three competitors.
- Paid e-card players use three pricing structures:
  - all-you-can-send subscriptions (AG, BM, JL, Greetings Island, Doozy);
  - pay-per-card (Moonpig £0.99, group cards $5.99+);
  - pay-per-event or per-guest (Evite, Paperless Post).
- Free tiers are either ad-funded (123Greetings), capped (Paperless Post: 50 free invitations) or short trials (AG 7 days, Punchbowl 7 days, Doozy 10 days). Doozy's trial is linked to many billing complaints.
- Prices shown as "promo" (Punchbowl at −50%) and App Store prices that differ from older web prices suggest heavy promotion. App Store IAP prices are the most reliable 2026 data point.
- For a premium animated-bouquet product, the incumbents set these reference points: about $1 per single digital card (Moonpig), $5–$10 per collaborative card or board, and about $24–$36/year for unlimited animated e-cards.

### Gaps

## Q2. Which French and European players exist, and how do they price (free vs premium/membership)?

### Takeaway
French "carte virtuelle" sites (Dromadaire, Cybercartes, Joliecarte) are **free and ad-funded**. None of them has an e-card subscription. They make money from ads, printed cards and gifts. The French paid players (Merci Facteur, Fizzer, Popcarte) sell **physical** cards ordered online, at roughly €1–€4.42 per card including postage, or by credits. I found no French-native paid animated e-card or group-card subscription. Within Europe, paid e-card subscriptions come from UK/US players with EUR pricing (Jacquie Lawson €30/yr) and from Moonpig/Greetz (eCards at €0.99).

### Inferences
- In France, the "carte virtuelle" has been free since the mid-2000s; Cybercartes went free in 2006. The money is in printed cards (Dromadaire from €0.99; Merci Facteur about €4.42 all-in; Fizzer about €2–2.89 per credit) and in gifts (Kisseo). A paid French animated-card or bouquet offer would compete with free habits. It would also have no direct French premium competitor, because the only premium subscriptions open to French users are anglophone (Jacquie Lawson at €30/yr).
- Dromadaire claimed 10–15M users or unique visitors (2011–2018), against an estimated ~197K visits in August 2026 (Semrush; see Q3). The metrics are not directly comparable, but the order-of-magnitude fall points to a sharp decline of free French e-card sites.
- Rising French postage (+7.4% in 2026) widens the cost gap between physical cards and digital ones.

### Gaps
- I found no current (2024–2026) audience or revenue figures for Dromadaire, Aventers or Cybercartes.
- I could not capture Popcarte's per-card price.
- Fizzer's credit prices conflict between promo sites; I found no official price list.
- I found no French-native group-card (carte collective) product comparable to Kudoboard.
- I did not research other European e-card players (Germany, Spain, Italy), Funky Pigeon, thortful or Card Factory (UK).
- I could not confirm whether Kisseo still offers e-cards; it now appears to sell personalized gifts.

## Q3. What public metrics exist (subscribers, users, revenue, traffic, acquisitions)?

### Takeaway
Of the incumbents, only **Moonpig Group plc** publishes audited figures: FY26 revenue £373.0m, 12.3m active customers and 1.2m Plus/Greetz Plus members. It does not break out eCard revenue. **American Greetings is private.** Its digital unit (AG Interactive: American Greetings, Blue Mountain, **Jacquie Lawson**, SmashUps, Creatacard) publishes no subscriber or revenue data. UK company records show that Jacquie Lawson's operating company (Microcourt Ltd) has been controlled by AG Interactive / AG's owners since at least 2016, and by Paul Singer (Elliott) since 3 Feb 2025. Other metrics are self-reported, such as Paperless Post's 200M users and Evite's ~3B invitations, or come from traffic estimates.

## Q4. Which players offer animated flower bouquets, music, video or personalized/unique art, and how is music handled?

### Takeaway
Incumbents offer flowers as a **card category or theme**, not as a bouquet you build yourself. Two exceptions are flower categories at free sites (123Greetings, Cybercartes) and new entrants such as VivoRose, an animated SVG bouquet builder. Premium positioning rests on **original art plus originally composed music** (Jacquie Lawson, Doozy) or on **personalization**: video greetings that name the recipient (AG/BM SmashUps), photo/video uploads (Creatacard, JL Postbox, Moonpig), AI-written messages and custom songs. Music comes from in-house compositions, curated libraries the user picks from, paid custom songs (Songfinch add-on, $6.99), or user uploads. I found no evidence that anyone licenses commercial chart music in e-cards.

### Inferences
- Music is handled in four ways:

## Q5. What trends affect these companies (e-card decline or growth, group cards, digital gifting add-ons, AI, mobile messaging)?

### Takeaway
Classic e-cards are a **mature, consolidating and shrinking** category:
- Hallmark exited in 2021.
- AG Interactive now owns the three leading paid brands under Elliott.
- French free e-card sites have lost most of their once-huge audiences.

Growth has moved to personalization and AI, group cards for workplaces, gifting attachments, and membership programmes. Delivery is shifting from email to SMS, WhatsApp and messaging apps.

### Cited Findings
- **Decline and consolidation.**
  - Hallmark closed eCards on 30 Apr 2021, citing "strengthening competition in the electronic cards arena" (a 2019 report) and a move to new digital projects — [Ingram's](https://ingrams.com/article/hallmark-cards-to-end-ecard-business-in-april/).
  - AG's digital brands (American Greetings, Blue Mountain, Jacquie Lawson, SmashUps, Creatacard) came under Elliott control in Feb 2025 — [Elliott release](https://kyodonewsprwire.jp/release/202502043742).

### Inferences
- Standalone "send an animated e-card" is a declining, consolidated niche. The value has moved to:
  - (a) personalization layers (video, names, AI text, custom songs);
  - (b) social and workplace group formats;
  - (c) monetizing a gift attached to the greeting.
- A digital flower-bouquet product fits these growth vectors better if it is collaborative (group bouquets), gift-attachable and shareable through messaging apps, than if it is a plain e-card.
- In France, free ad-funded e-card habits and the lack of a paid local incumbent mean pricing must beat "free" on perceived value. Likely levers are unique, art-quality animation, personalization, and a real or digital gift attachment. Moonpig's £0.99 eCard sets a low consumer anchor for a one-off digital card.

- I found no data on what share of greetings now go through WhatsApp or iMessage stickers versus e-cards.
- I found no AI adoption metrics for AG or Jacquie Lawson.
- A claim that "60% of consumers prefer AI-personalized cards" appeared only via a low-quality aggregator and is excluded as unreliable.
