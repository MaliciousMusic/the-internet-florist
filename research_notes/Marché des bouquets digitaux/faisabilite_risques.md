# Feasibility, differentiation and risks: a France-based online shop selling procedurally generated animated digital bouquets (as of October 2026)

Research date: 2 October 2026. Scope: pricing benchmarks and models, unit economics, music legal and IP constraints (France/EU), payments and tax, consumer law, hosting, acquisition and CAC, competition, defensibility, and lessons from comparable digital-gift companies. Unless stated otherwise, figures were taken from the cited page as fetched in late September or early October 2026. Where a figure came only from a search-result summary and not from an opened page, it is flagged.

---

## 1. Which price points and pricing models work for small personalized digital gifts?

### Takeaway
Comparable digital gifts fall into three price bands. (a) Free: viral bouquet generators and the TikTok "Flower Language" filter. (b) Roughly $5–$25 one-off: Kudoboard boards at $6.99/$9.99/$19.99, Etsy animated e-cards at about $5–$8, and AI song gifts at $4.90–$24.99. (c) About €30/$36 a year for unlimited-use subscriptions from animated e-card incumbents (Jacquie Lawson, American Greetings). Premium human-made personalization can command $199 (Songfinch). Two models fit the market best: a tiered per-item model (cheap or free entry, with paid upsells for music, premium flowers, export and scheduling), and optionally an annual "unlimited" pass priced against the €30/yr incumbents. Free generators set the price anchor at zero.

### Cited Findings
**Per-item and tiered models**
- Kudoboard per-board prices (current page): **Lite $6.99** (up to 20 posts; messages, photos and GIFs; gift-card attachment), **Premium $9.99** (up to 100 posts, video, download), **Milestone $19.99** (unlimited posts, custom background uploads, slideshow playback), **Memorial $99** — [Kudoboard pricing](https://www.kudoboard.com/pricing)
- Earlier listings showed Kudoboard Lite at $5.99 and Premium at $8.99 (Milestone $19.99), which suggests a ~$1 rise on the entry tiers (search-result summary) — [Capterra Kudoboard pricing 2026](https://www.capterra.com/p/182991/Kudoboard/pricing/)
- Kudoboard team subscriptions: Business $25–39/month or $299/yr; Pro $38–59/month or $449/yr; Enterprise custom. This shows that a B2B/group-celebration tier sits next to per-item consumer pricing — [Kudoboard pricing](https://www.kudoboard.com/pricing)
- Songfinch: custom human-written and recorded song for **$199**, delivered in 3–7 days. The buyer gets "a Personal Use license in perpetuity", and optional add-ons raise the order value — [Songfinch support: Pricing and Payment Options](https://support.songfinch.com/hc/en-us/articles/17016466252571-Pricing-and-Payment-Options)
- AI song gifts (2026 launches): Songive charges **$4.90 one-time** for a ~3-minute AI song generated in ~2 minutes in 20 languages — [Barchart / ACCESS Newswire](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/3067892/songive-launches-ai-powered-platform-that-turns-personal-stories-into-custom-songs-in-minutes). CustomSong charges **$24.99 one-time** with a free preview — [SimilarLabs](https://similarlabs.com/p/customsong-ai-music-generator). WrapASong starts at **$24.99** — [Capterra WrapASong](https://www.capterra.com/p/10043899/WrapASong/)
- Etsy digital gifts (search-result summary; prices vary with sales and currency): animated photo birthday e-card $5.64; animated birthday Canva-template e-cards $4.87–$7.99; watercolor bouquet clipart $1.63 (on sale from $4.08) — [Etsy listing example](https://www.etsy.com/listing/1790879359)

**Subscription models (incumbent animated e-cards)**
- Jacquie Lawson (animated e-cards, the closest incumbent analogue): **$36 for 1 year, $50 for 2 years; €30/yr; £27/yr**, with unlimited sending of the full range during membership — [Jacquie Lawson membership](https://www.jacquielawson.com/prices-membership)
- American Greetings e-cards: yearly subscription **$35.99** (App Store pricing data, 2026) — [AppPricingLab](https://apppricinglab.com/app/apple/1612834659). Membership starts at $6.99 for one month, or as little as $1.67/month on a 2-year plan (secondary roundup) — [Greenvelope roundup](https://uniko.greenvelope.com/resources/best-digital-greeting-card-sites)

**Free anchors and freemium benchmarks**
- DigiBouquet (digibouquet.net): free; 8 illustrated flowers, a styled message card and a shareable link; "No accounts, no downloads, no fees" — [About DigiBouquet](https://digibouquet.net/about)
- The original Digibouquet, by illustrator and content creator Pauline Wee, is described as an entirely free platform for sending illustrated digital bouquets by link (Artribune, 14 Feb 2026) — [Artribune](https://www.artribune.com/editoria/grafica-illustrazione/2026/02/fiori-digitali-illustrati-digibouquet/)
- Freemium conversion benchmark: freemium apps have a median free-to-paid conversion of **2.18%** (top decile 6–8%), against **12.11%** for hard-paywall apps (RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps data; search-result summary) — [RevenueCat 2026 benchmarks](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026.md); [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/freemium-to-premium-conversion-techniques)

### Inferences
- Zero-priced bouquet generators and $4.90 AI songs cap what an entry product can charge. A plausible structure: a free or very cheap base (€0–3.99), a premium tier at about €7.99–9.99 (music, rare or "hundreds of" species, richer animation, scheduled delivery), and a top tier at about €14.99–19.99 (HD video or keepsake export, multiple recipients, a print add-on). This mirrors Kudoboard's $6.99 → $9.99 → $19.99 ladder, where video, download and custom backgrounds justify price steps of roughly 1.4x to 3x.
- An annual "unlimited" pass at about €19–29/yr would undercut Jacquie Lawson (€30/yr) and American Greetings ($35.99/yr). Occasion-driven buyers may not renew, though, so subscriptions should be an option, not the core model.
- French Valentine's flower buyers spend about €25.20 per household (see Q4). A €5–10 digital bouquet is therefore 20–40% of a physical bouquet budget. It can be positioned as a cheaper, instant, long-distance or last-minute alternative, or as an add-on to a physical gift.
- Songfinch's $199 shows that buyers pay steeply for perceived human craft and emotional depth. Claims such as "unique composition", a certificate of uniqueness or human-illustrated flowers may support higher prices than "generated" positioning would.

### Gaps
- Cameo per-video prices and digital-art commission prices were not collected.
- No data was found on attach rates or prices for e-gift add-ons sold alongside physical gifts (for example florists' digital cards), or on willingness to pay for AR upsells.
- No published A/B evidence was found on per-item versus subscription pricing for digital bouquets.

## 2. How can music be legally included in a paid product sold from France/EU, and what are the risks?

### Takeaway
Music is the highest legal-risk component. Popular copyrighted songs need authorization for both the composition (SACEM in France) and the master recording (producers/labels). In practice that is out of reach for a micro-startup, and using them without authorization is criminal infringement ("contrefaçon": up to 3 years' prison and a €300,000 fine). Standard creator plans from mainstream royalty-free libraries forbid exactly this use case: Artlist bans "video loop animation combined with Artlist music in the background", templates, standalone use, and apps used by end-users without an Enterprise deal. Epidemic Sound's standard licenses require music to be synchronized in your own production, and product integrations go through its developer/Partner API. AI music is usable commercially on Suno paid plans, but with no copyright warranty, new download caps (from 3 Sept 2026), and a Munich court loss to GEMA (31 July 2026). Udio has disabled downloads. The safest routes are commissioned tracks with a full rights buy-out from non-SACEM composers (or catalogs "hors gestion collective"), or a negotiated enterprise/API license.

### Cited Findings
**SACEM and collective management (France)**
- A contract with SACEM is not mandatory if you use only music whose authors or composers died more than 70 years ago. Several companies also sell paid catalogs of music outside collective management ("hors gestion collective", i.e. non-SACEM) — [Service-public Entreprendre F3094](https://entreprendre.service-public.gouv.fr/vosdroits/F3094); [justice.fr](https://www.justice.fr/fiche/diffuser-musique-commerce-sacem)
- For physical shops, the SACEM "contrat général de représentation" runs for 1 year and renews tacitly. Invoices come from SACEM and SPRE, and the minimum annual bill is €113.45 HT per establishment. These are rules for premises, given only for reference, not a web tariff — [Service-public F3094](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/entreprendre/vosdroits/F3094)
- SACEM publishes separate general authorization conditions for on-demand online listening or viewing of works "à l'unité" — [SACEM CGA via LawInsider](https://www.lawinsider.com/fr/contracts/555OqLKUExG). It also has flat tariffs for small non-commercial webradios: €80 HT/yr without podcasts and €120 HT with podcasts, if revenue or expenses are ≤ €20,000 (2021 flyer) — [SACEM Webradio/Podcast flyer 2021](https://www.editionmultimedia.fr/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Flyer-Sacem-Webradio-Podcast-2021.pdf)
- Infringement penalties (CPI art. L335-2 and related): 3 years' imprisonment and a €300,000 fine; 7 years and €750,000 when committed by an organized group — [Légifrance CPI L335-2](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/id/LEGIARTI000006279166/2006-08-03); [Swim.legal](https://www.swim.legal/blog/contrefacon-droits-auteur-risques-sanctions-protections)

**Royalty-free libraries**
- Artlist license (Social updated 4 Jan 2026; Pro/Business 15 Feb 2026; plans Social, Pro, Max Business, Enterprise). Assets may be used only "as integrated elements within broader Projects", not as standalone content. Resale is barred "in any way", including templates and libraries. Quote: "The creation of a static video, a video loop animation combined with Artlist music in the background or lyric video with Artlist music is also forbidden." Use in "App, Software, games and their end-users" requires an Enterprise agreement — [Artlist License](https://help-center.artlist.io/privacy-terms/artlist-license)
- Epidemic Sound: the Personal/Creator plan is non-commercial and not for business or client work; the Commercial plan covers business use (search-result summary) — [Epidemic Sound commercial subscription](https://www.epidemicsound.com/commercial-subscription/); [personal subscription](https://www.epidemicsound.com/personal-subscription/)
- Epidemic Sound's (legacy) single-track license prohibits making licensed works available "on a standalone basis, meaning without being synchronized with a video production". Music is meant for the background of your own production (search-result summary of the PDF) — [Epidemic Sound Single Track Licenses V9 (PDF)](https://www.epidemicsound.com/staticfiles/legacy/20/documents/SingleTrackLicensesV9.pdf)
- For products whose end-users pick and use music, Epidemic Sound offers a developer/Partner API. It has a free tier with "Full catalog access for prototyping and evaluation". Paid tiers (Scale, and Enterprise with a custom license) have no public prices. Music is described as "cleared for commercial use and publishing across platforms" — [Epidemic Sound for developers](https://www.epidemicsound.com/business/developers/)

**AI music generators**
- Suno Terms of Service: free-tier output is limited to personal, non-commercial use. Paid Pro/Premier subscribers get commercial rights, with Suno assigning "all of its right, title and interest" in outputs. Suno "makes no representation or warranty to you that any copyright will vest in any Output". Outputs may not be used "to compete with Suno" — [ConductAtlas summary of Suno ToS](https://conductatlas.com/platform/suno/suno-terms-of-service/commercial-use-restrictions-by-subscription-tier/); [Terms.law](https://terms.law/ai-output-rights/suno/)
- Suno policy update (blog post dated 10 Aug 2026; effective 3 Sept 2026):
  - Free tier: up to 7 lifetime trial downloads, "not eligible for commercial use".
  - Pro: 20 downloads/month. Premier: 60/month. Premier + Suno Studio: unlimited. Extra downloads can be bought.
  - "Songs downloaded from Suno on paid plans remain yours to use commercially or personally."
  - Models are described as "developed in partnership with the music industry".
  - [Suno blog: An update to our downloads policy and Terms of Service](https://about.suno.com/blog/suno-updates-tos)
- GEMA v. Suno: on **31 July 2026** the Munich Regional Court (42nd Civil Chamber) largely upheld GEMA's claims. It ordered Suno to stop reproducing protected works and using them for training, to disclose related revenue and to pay damages, with a fine of up to €250,000 per future breach. Six well-known works (for example "Daddy Cool", "Forever Young") were found to be "reproducibly contained" in Suno's v3.5 and v4 models — [JUVE Patent](https://juve-patent.com/cases/munich-regional-court-stops-suno-using-gema-protected-music); [CMS AI case tracker](https://cms.law/en/hrv/publication/artificial-intelligence-and-copyright-case-tracker/germany-gema-v.-suno-31-july-2026)
- Udio: under its UMG partnership (announced 29 Oct 2025), downloads of audio, video and stems were disabled, with only a brief 48-hour download window. In 2026 Udio is to be a "walled garden" where users can stream creations but not take them to other platforms. Warner Music Group settled with Udio in November 2025 and signed a licensing deal for a 2026 platform — [Yahoo/AP](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/ai-song-generator-udio-offers-132621881.html); [Annielytics Udio timeline](https://www.annielytics.com/tools/ai-timeline/company/udio/)

### Inferences
- Ranking of options by legal safety and practicality:
  1. **Commissioned short loops (for example 20–100 tracks)** from independent composers, with a written assignment of rights covering online communication to the public and reproduction, worldwide and for the duration of copyright, as French law requires each assigned right to be specified. Check that the composers are not SACEM members: members generally entrust their rights to SACEM, which would bring the use back under SACEM licensing. This needs confirmation with SACEM.
  2. **Catalogs explicitly "hors gestion collective"** (service-public says these exist), but only with a license that expressly allows embedding in a product sold per unit to end customers.
  3. **Epidemic Sound Partner API** (negotiated license) if per-user music choice matters. Pricing is unknown.
  4. **Suno paid-plan output** is legally usable under its terms. However, there is no copyright protection or exclusivity, so competitors can copy the tracks. The EU legal climate is uncertain after the GEMA ruling, and there is reputational risk ("AI music" in an emotional gift). Download caps of 20–60 per month are manageable for building a fixed library over a few months.
  5. **Public-domain compositions** (composers dead >70 years): the compositions are free, but recordings carry separate neighbouring rights. You would need your own recordings or explicitly free-licensed recordings. This is general legal knowledge, not verified in this research.
- **Avoid** letting buyers attach copyrighted songs (for example "our song") as hosted audio. That makes the shop a distributor or host of infringing content. Embedding an official player (Spotify/YouTube) is a possible workaround, but those platforms' embed terms were not researched.
- With standard Artlist or Epidemic subscriptions, the "optional music" upsell on a looping animation is very likely non-compliant (Artlist's ban on "video loop animation ... with music in the background" is explicit). Music licensing should be budgeted as a fixed cost (commissioning) and not assumed to be covered by a ~€10–20/month creator subscription.

### Gaps
- No SACEM tariff was found for music embedded in paid personalized web content or e-greetings. SACEM should be asked directly about any obligation if all music is non-SACEM.
- Paid prices and terms of the Epidemic Sound Partner API are not public.
- The Epidemic Sound help-center article on allowed uses returned HTTP 403, so the current (non-legacy) subscription wording on "products sold to end customers" was not verified.
- No authoritative French/EU source was gathered on the copyrightability of AI-generated music or on SACEM's policy toward AI-generated works.
- No benchmark was found for the cost of commissioning short music loops from independent composers.

## 3. What does selling digital products from France require and cost (VAT/OSS, merchant of record, consumer law, hosting)?

### Takeaway
A French micro-entrepreneur can start with no VAT under the "franchise en base" (€37,500 for services in 2026) as long as cross-border B2C sales of e-services to other EU countries stay under the EU-wide €10,000 threshold. Above it, destination-country VAT applies through the OSS one-stop shop. Payment options trade margin for simplicity:
- **Stripe direct**: 1.5% + €0.25 on EEA standard cards, the cheapest.
- **Stripe Managed Payments** (merchant of record, MoR): +3.5% on top of processing.
- **Paddle and Lemon Squeezy** (MoR): 5% + 50¢, with Lemon Squeezy adding +1.5% for non-US transactions.
- **Gumroad** (MoR): 10% + 50¢.

At €5–10 tickets, the fixed 50¢ makes MoRs expensive. On consumer law, the 14-day withdrawal right can be excluded for immediately supplied digital content only with express prior consent plus an express waiver. Since 19 June 2026 a withdrawal button is mandatory where the right exists, and one-off digital content carries a 2-year conformity guarantee. Hosting procedurally generated bouquets as seeds is close to free (Cloudflare R2: free egress, 10 GB free storage). Vercel's free Hobby plan cannot be used commercially.

### Cited Findings
**VAT, OSS and micro-entreprise**
- Since 1 July 2021 a single EU-wide threshold of **€10,000 HT/yr** covers intra-EU B2C distance sales plus telecom, broadcasting and electronic services. Above it, the seller must charge the VAT of the customer's Member State. OSS registration is optional below the threshold. In France it is done in the professional space on impots.gouv.fr ("Guichet de TVA UE") — [Dougs](https://www.dougs.fr/blog/e-commerce-en-union-europeenne-le-point-sur-le-guichet-unique-oss-ioss-de-tva/); [Ecommerce Nation](https://www.ecommerce-nation.fr/guichet-unique-tva/); [ClearTax FR](https://www.cleartax.com/fr/guichet-unique-oss)
- French "franchise en base de TVA" in 2026: **€37,500** for services, with a "seuil majoré" of **€41,250**. Exceeding the majoré threshold makes VAT due from the date it is crossed; exceeding only the base threshold applies VAT from 1 January of the next year. Invoices must state "TVA non applicable, article 293 B du CGI". The planned single threshold (€25,000) was abandoned: article 25 of the PLF 2026 was rejected and the thresholds remain unchanged — [Socic](https://www.socic.fr/ressources-comptabilite/articles/micro-entreprise-et-tva-en-2026-franchise-depassement-de-seuils-regime-reel-et-option-volontaire/); [Abby](https://abby.fr/blog/reforme-tva-autoentrepreneur/)
- Micro-entreprise in 2026: turnover ceiling **€83,600** for services (BIC or BNC). Social contribution rates: **21.2%** for BIC services; **25.6%** for BNC liberal professions outside CIPAV (up from 24.6%); 23.2% for CIPAV — [Hayot Expertise](https://hayot-expertise.fr/blog/micro-entreprise-plafonds-regles-2026); [PropulseByCA](https://propulsebyca.fr/actualites/nouveaux-plafonds-chiffre-affaires-2026-micro-entreprise)

**Payment processors and merchants of record**
- Stripe France ([Stripe FR pricing](https://stripe.com/fr/pricing)):
  - Cards: EEA standard **1.5% + €0.25**; EEA premium 2.8% + €0.25; UK 2.5% + €0.25; international 3.15% + €0.25, plus 2% for currency conversion.
  - Stripe Tax: 0.5% per transaction (no-code), or €0.45 per transaction via API.
  - Stripe Managed Payments (MoR): **3.5% per successful transaction, on top of standard processing fees**.
- Conflicting source: a competitor's blog states Stripe Managed Payments costs "5% + $0.50" — [Dodo Payments](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/lemonsqueezy-alternatives). The official Stripe FR page (3.5% + processing, i.e. about 5% + €0.25 on EEA standard cards) should be preferred.
- Lemon Squeezy (MoR): **5% + 50¢** per transaction, with no monthly fee — [Lemon Squeezy pricing](https://www.lemonsqueezy.com/pricing). Surcharges: **+1.5% for international (non-US) transactions**, +1.5% for PayPal, +0.5% for subscription payments. Payouts via Stripe cost 1% to non-US bank accounts. Abandoned-cart recovery adds +5% and affiliate referrals +3% — [Lemon Squeezy fees docs](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/getting-started/fees)
- Lemon Squeezy platform risk: Stripe acquired Lemon Squeezy in July 2024. In January 2026 Lemon Squeezy acknowledged slower support and fewer updates, and Stripe Managed Payments entered public preview in February 2026 as the migration path. Some Lemon Squeezy features (digital download delivery, storefront builder, affiliates) reportedly are not part of Stripe Managed Payments. These sources are competitor blogs and may be biased — [Dodo Payments](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/lemonsqueezy-alternatives); [Fungies](https://fungies.io/lemon-squeezy-stripe-acquisition-saas-founders-2026/)
- Paddle (MoR): **5% + 50¢** per checkout transaction, including MoR services and VAT/sales-tax compliance. "If you're selling product with under 10$ value you can contact us for bespoke pricing" — [Paddle pricing](https://www.paddle.com/pricing)
- Gumroad (MoR since **1 January 2025**, handling VAT and GST): **10% + $0.50** on direct sales; **30%** on sales through the Discover marketplace — [Gumroad pricing](https://gumroad.com/pricing)
- Etsy France (secondary sources):
  - Fees: $0.20 listing fee (charged per unit sold on multi-quantity digital listings); 6.5% transaction fee; payment processing 4% + €0.30 (most EU countries); France "regulatory operating fee" 0.47% — [Crosslist](https://crosslist.com/blog/etsy-review); [Outfy](https://www.outfy.com/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-sell-on-etsy)
  - A June 2026 increase to the regulatory operating fee has been reported, so the current France rate is unverified — [Beancount.io](https://beancount.io/uk/blog/2026/08/22/etsy-regulatory-operating-fee-increase-june-2026-seller-payout-bookkeeping-guide)

**Consumer law (France/EU)**
- Right of withdrawal is 14 days. For digital content not supplied on a tangible medium, the consumer loses it only if performance began after their **prior express consent and express waiver** (Code de la consommation L221-28). A pre-ticked box is not enough, and consent cannot be inferred from accepting the T&Cs or from payment alone — [LegalPlace](https://www.legalplace.fr/guides/renoncement-expres-droit-retractation); [Donneespersonnelles.fr](https://donneespersonnelles.fr/contenu-numerique-acces-immediat); [LegalPlace guide](https://www.legalplace.fr/guides/droit-retractation-internet/)
- Mandatory online withdrawal function: Directive (EU) 2023/2673 adds Art. 11a to the Consumer Rights Directive, applying from **19 June 2026**. France transposed it by **ordonnance n° 2026-2 of 5 January 2026**, amending L221-21 and codified at D221-5.
  - Requirements: a clearly labeled function, e.g. "renoncer au contrat ici", that is easy to find and available throughout the 14-day period, with acknowledgment on a durable medium such as email.
  - Contracts with no statutory withdrawal right are not covered.
  - Sources: [Ecommerce Nation](https://www.ecommerce-nation.fr/e-commerce-le-bouton-de-retractation-devient-obligatoire-le-19-juin-2026/); [Bbox-mag](https://www.bbox-mag.fr/mobiles/forfaits-et-services/droit-retractation-en-ligne/46449959); [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/eu-consumer-protection-law-update-new-mandatory-withdrawal-button-what-online-traders-need-to-know); [Greenberg Traurig, May 2026](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/5/eu-consumer-law-new-withdrawal-button-requirements-for-online-contracts)
- Ecommerce Nation reports fines of up to €75,000 per company for non-compliance, and withdrawal periods extended by up to 12 months when information is missing. **Not verified on Légifrance**, so treat the amount with caution — [Ecommerce Nation](https://www.ecommerce-nation.fr/e-commerce-le-bouton-de-retractation-devient-obligatoire-le-19-juin-2026/)
- Legal guarantee of conformity for digital content and services (France, transposing the Digital Content Directive): a **single supply carries a 2-year guarantee**, with defects presumed pre-existing for 1 year. Content must match its description and functionality, and remedies are repair or replacement, then a price reduction or refund — [Eurojuris](https://www.eurojuris.fr/articles/extension-garantie-legale-conformite-aux-services-numeriques-40728.htm); [Soulier Avocats](https://soulier-avocats.com/la-nouvelle-garantie-legale-de-conformite-pour-les-contenus-et-services-numeriques/?pdf=42973)
- European Accessibility Act: micro-enterprises **providing services** (fewer than 10 employees and turnover or balance sheet ≤ €2M) are exempt (Art. 4(5)). E-commerce is otherwise in scope. France's décret n° 2026-816 of 24 Aug 2026 aligned the national rules with the directive — [Xictron](https://www.xictron.com/en/blog/accessibility-act-exemptions-microenterprises-2026/); [Askem](https://askem.com/compliance/eaa/)

**Hosting for "permanent links"**
- Cloudflare R2: storage $0.015/GB-month; Class A operations $4.50/million; Class B $0.36/million. Free tier each month: 10 GB-month storage, 1M Class A and 10M Class B operations. **Egress is free** — [Cloudflare R2 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/)
- Vercel (docs updated 14 Sept 2026): "Hobby teams are restricted to non-commercial personal use only". Any method of processing payment from visitors counts as commercial and requires Pro or Enterprise. Hobby includes 100 GB Fast Data Transfer and 1M function invocations per month — [Vercel Fair Use Guidelines](https://vercel.com/docs/limits/fair-use-guidelines)

### Inferences
**Worked fee comparison.** Assumptions: displayed VAT-inclusive prices, a French customer at 20% VAT, an EEA standard card, and $0.50 ≈ €0.45 (an assumed exchange rate). All fee rates are from the sources above.

| Route (€9.99 sale) | VAT | Fees | Net to seller | % of sticker |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stripe direct, franchise en base (no VAT) | 0 | €0.40 | **€9.59** | 96% |
| Stripe direct + Stripe Tax, VAT-registered | €1.665 | €0.40 + €0.05 | **€7.88** | 79% |
| Stripe Managed Payments (3.5% + 1.5% + €0.25) | €1.665 | €0.75 | **€7.58** | 76% |
| Paddle (5% + $0.50) | €1.665 | ~€0.95 | **~€7.38** | 74% |
| Lemon Squeezy (5% + 1.5% intl + $0.50, + 1% payout) | €1.665 | ~€1.17 | **~€7.15** | 72% |
| Gumroad (10% + $0.50) | €1.665 | ~€1.45 | **~€6.88** | 69% |
| Etsy (listing + 6.5% + 4% + €0.30 + 0.47%), VAT not modeled | n/a | ~€1.58 | n/a | fees ≈ 16% |

At €4.99, net is about €4.67 (Stripe under the franchise), €3.81 (Stripe, VAT-registered), €3.66 (Stripe Managed Payments), €3.46 (Paddle), €3.35 (Lemon Squeezy) and €3.21 (Gumroad). The fixed $0.50 component takes 9–10% of a €4.99 sticker price on its own, which argues for prices ≥ €7.99, bundles, or credit packs, or Paddle's bespoke pricing for items under $10.

**Other inferences**
- **Using an MoR forfeits the franchise-en-base advantage at checkout.** MoRs charge consumer VAT from the first sale, because the MoR is the seller to the consumer. The French business then invoices the MoR (B2B). This simplifies compliance, especially for UK, US or Swiss buyers, at a cost of about 4–8 margin points. It needs validation by an accountant.
- **Lowest-cost start** (if sales are mostly in France or the EU): micro-entreprise, Stripe direct, and the franchise en base while French turnover is < €37,500 and cross-border EU B2C e-services are < €10,000. Register for OSS when the €10,000 threshold is crossed. Non-EU sales (UK, US, CH) have their own digital-services tax rules, which were not researched and which favour an MoR.
- **Illustrative micro-entreprise P&L** (sourced inputs): 1,000 orders × €9.99 via Stripe under the franchise gives turnover ≈ €9,990. Payment fees are ≈ €400 and BIC-services social contributions are 21.2% of turnover ≈ €2,118. That leaves ≈ €7,470 before music, art, software, ads and income tax.
- **Procedural generation makes "permanent links" cheap.** If each bouquet is stored as seed, parameters and message (a few KB), 1M bouquets is a few GB, which fits within R2's 10 GB free tier with free egress. The marginal hosting cost per bouquet is effectively zero; costs are the domain, the app hosting plan (a paid plan is needed if using Vercel) and the music files.
- **The "permanent link" promise is a legal commitment.** A one-off digital supply carries at least the 2-year conformity guarantee, and a "forever" claim creates a contractual expectation. Mitigations: state a guaranteed minimum duration (for example 10 years), provide a downloadable keepsake (MP4/GIF or self-contained HTML), and plan for continuity if the business closes.
- **Checkout design:**
  - Use an unticked checkbox: "Je demande l'accès immédiat au contenu et je reconnais perdre mon droit de rétractation", plus a confirmation email on a durable medium.
  - If buyers can **schedule** delivery (for example ordering on 1 Feb for 14 Feb), performance has not begun, so the withdrawal right and the mandatory withdrawal button likely apply until delivery. Implement the button anyway. This is a legal interpretation to confirm with counsel.
- The microenterprise exemption likely removes EAA accessibility obligations at launch, but they apply once the business exceeds 10 staff or €2M.

### Gaps
- Whether Gumroad's 10% + $0.50 includes card-processing fees was not verified (its help article did not load).
- Eligibility of France-based sellers for Stripe Managed Payments, which was in public preview, was not verified.
- VAT and GST obligations for non-EU buyers (UK, US, Switzerland, Canada) were not researched.
- The micro-entreprise classification of selling digital bouquets ("prestation de services BIC" vs other categories) was not confirmed with an official source.

## 4. How do similar products acquire customers, how seasonal is demand, and what conversion rates and CAC are typical?

### Takeaway
Flower gifting in France is strongly seasonal, and online purchasing is rising. About 1.3M households bought flowers or plants for Saint-Valentin 2025 (€25.20 average), with the online share doubling to 13%, and 2.2M households bought for Fête des mères 2025 (€61.8M). Viral precedent exists: the TikTok "Flower Language" bouquet filter had more than 3.5M uses in days, and free bouquet sites draw press coverage on Valentine's Day. Paid acquisition economics are poor for a low-ticket item. French Meta CPCs of €0.40–1.80 and e-commerce conversion of about 2–3% imply a CAC of roughly €13–90 per first purchase, against about €7–8 net per €9.99 order. The business must rely on organic and viral loops (every bouquet is seen by its recipient), creator content and seasonal SEO, with paid media used only to amplify peaks.

### Cited Findings
**Seasonality and market size (France)**

  - **1.3M households** bought flowers or plants, the same as 2024.
  - **Average budget €25.20 per household** (€25.30 in 2024).
  - Roses were 57% of purchases (58% in 2024).
  - 75% of spend went to ready-made bunches or bouquets (46% in 2024).

### Inferences
- **Implied paid CAC:**
  - Meta France: CPC €0.40–1.80 ÷ conversion 2–3% ≈ **€13–90 per first purchase**.
  - TikTok: CPC ~$0.30–1.50 ÷ 2–3% ≈ $10–75.
  - Even the best case (€13) exceeds the ~€3.4–7.9 net per order (Q3).
  - Paid ads only work for retargeting, high-intent peak days, higher-value bundles or annual passes, or amplifying content that is already working organically.
- **Built-in viral loop**: every paid bouquet is opened by at least one recipient. A "create your own" call to action on the recipient page, a tasteful "made with ..." signature and a free tier make each sale a distribution event. This is the main lever free bouquet sites have used. The key metrics to test are recipient-to-creator conversion and shares per bouquet.
- **Seasonal plan (France):** Saint-Valentin (14 Feb), 1er mai, Fête des mères (last Sunday of May; 31 May 2026), Fête des pères (June), Christmas/New Year, plus always-on birthdays and anniversaries.
  - Digital delivery is instant, which suits **last-minute** and **long-distance** gifting and the "forgot a gift" search intent on the day.
  - The rising online share of flower purchases (6% → 13% for Valentine's) shows French buyers' growing comfort with buying flowers online.
- **Organic channels to prioritize:** short-form video of the bouquet "blooming" with music (the product is inherently visual), creator seeding around peaks, and French SEO landing pages per occasion and per flower species. "Hundreds of flower types" can generate long-tail pages such as "language of flowers" and "meaning of the peony". Kudoboard's existence also suggests a B2B or group angle (teams, events) with higher order values.
- Demand is concentrated on a few days, so infrastructure, payments and support must handle spikes. Monthly averages will understate peak-load needs.

### Gaps
- No public CAC or ROAS benchmark specific to low-ticket digital gifts or e-cards was found. The CAC above is derived from CPC and conversion.
- France-specific TikTok ad costs, French micro-influencer prices and creator-seeding conversion data were not collected.
- Not available: DigiBouquet traffic or usage metrics, recipient-to-sender viral conversion rates for any digital-gift product, and data on the share of demand from birthdays versus fixed holidays.

## 5. What are the competitive threats and defensibility, and how have similar startups fared?

### Takeaway
The core mechanic (pick flowers, write a message, share a link) is already free and widely cloned: DigiBouquet and clones, plus several 2026 indie launches with $0 revenue. General AI tools commoditize "personalized animated content with music": Sora passed 1M downloads in under 5 days, and AI song gifts start at $4.90. Animated e-card incumbents offer unlimited sending for about €30/yr. Comparable companies show both sides of the category:
- **Songfinch** scaled human-made $199 songs (130,000 delivered by 2022).
- **Kudoboard** is bootstrapped and profitable-looking at $6.99–$19.99 per board.
- **Cameo** lost about 90% of its value after the novelty peak.
- **Hallmark** exited e-cards in 2021, and a charity e-card service closed for not covering its costs.

Defensibility cannot rest on the generator, which can be cloned in weeks. It must come from art quality and brand, a proprietary music catalog, seasonal marketing execution, recipient experience and keepsake features, and possibly B2B or florist partnerships.

### Cited Findings
**Free generators and indie clones**
- DigiBouquet (digibouquet.net, built and maintained by Tommy Tang): free, 8 illustrated flowers (roses, peonies, tulips, daisies, lilies, orchids, camellias, lotus), a message card and a single shareable link. "No accounts, no downloads, no fees" — [About DigiBouquet](https://digibouquet.net/about)
- The original Digibouquet, by South Korean illustrator and content creator Pauline Wee, is a free platform. The article gives no monetization information — [Artribune, 14 Feb 2026](https://www.artribune.com/editoria/grafica-illustrazione/2026/02/fiori-digitali-illustrati-digibouquet/)
- "Digital Flower Bouquet" (Indie Hackers; founder "Yaelon"; launched **11 June 2026**): free, no sign-up, shareable links, **self-reported revenue $0/mo** — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/digital-flower-bouquet)
- VivoRose (virtual bouquet with an animated envelope and blooming effect in hand-crafted SVG): **0 upvotes, #105 on the Product Hunt daily leaderboard**, not featured — [Hunted.space](https://hunted.space/product/vivorose). BouquetNote (watercolor bouquet or letter shared by private link or HD image download) also launched on Product Hunt — [Product Hunt BouquetNote](https://www.producthunt.com/products/bouquetnote/makers)

**AI tools**
- OpenAI's Sora app (launched 30 Sept 2025; 10-second clips from text prompts) passed **1M downloads in under 5 days**, faster than ChatGPT's launch, and reached #1 on the US App Store — [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/openai-sora-1-million-downloads); [OODA Loop](https://oodaloop.com/briefs/technology/openais-sora-hit-1-million-downloads-in-less-than-five-days)
- AI personalized songs are sold as gifts for $4.90 (Songive) to $24.99 (CustomSong, WrapASong); see Q1 — [Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/3067892/songive-launches-ai-powered-platform-that-turns-personal-stories-into-custom-songs-in-minutes)

**Incumbents**
- Jacquie Lawson (animated e-cards, €30/yr) and American Greetings ($35.99/yr); see Q1 — [Jacquie Lawson](https://www.jacquielawson.com/prices-membership); [AppPricingLab](https://apppricinglab.com/app/apple/1612834659)
- Hallmark ended its eCard business on **30 April 2021** after about 15 years, saying it would focus on "new digital capabilities" — [Ingram's](https://ingrams.com/article/hallmark-cards-to-end-ecard-business-in-april/)
- Word Forest (UK charity) closed its e-card service because it "didn't bring in enough to cover its running costs" — [Word Forest](https://www.wordforest.org/ecards)
- Florists are shifting online: the online share of Valentine's flower purchases went from 6% to 13% (2024 → 2025) — [Valhor (PDF)](https://bo.valhor.fr/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/cp-st-valentin_2026.pdf)

**Comparable digital-gift startups**
- Cameo (personalized celebrity videos):
  - $100M gross revenue in 2020, and a $1B+ valuation in 2021 after a $100M raise.
  - It then lost about 90% of that value by 2024, with revenue reportedly down more than 80% from peak (secondary source).
  - Staff fell from nearly 400 to fewer than 50 after layoff rounds in May 2022, November 2022 and July 2023.
  - In December 2024 it broadened toward a wider creator marketplace.
  - Sources: [Startups.co.uk](https://startups.co.uk/news/what-happened-to-cameo/); [TheWrap](https://thewrap.com/cameo-layoffs-50-employees-remain); [Everything-PR](https://everything-pr.com/cameo-the-celebrity-video-platform-that-lost-90-percent-of-its-value)
- Songfinch (personalized songs by human artists):
  - Raised a **$17M Series A on 29 Sept 2022** ($21.8M total at the time) at $199/song.
  - **130,000 songs delivered** to date at that point, and more than $12M paid to artists in the prior 12 months (average artist about $25k/yr).
  - Reported revenue growth of more than 27,000% over three years.
  - Source: [Music Business Worldwide](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/the-weeknd-backed-personalized-song-creation-firm-songfinch-raises-17m)
  - An aggregator lists $35.22M raised over 6 rounds, the latest $1.92M on 3 July 2025, and **2023 revenue of $75M**. The revenue figure is an unverified aggregator estimate — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/songfinch/financials)
- Kudoboard (group digital cards; founded 2015; CEO Aaron Rubens): described as bootstrapped (never raised), with about 21–50 employees and about **$2.3M annual revenue**. These are data-aggregator estimates surfaced in a search summary: **low confidence** — [Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/c/kudoboard); [Myshortlister (Aaron Rubens)](https://www.myshortlister.com/shortlister-experts/aaron-rubens)

**Legal headwinds for AI-based competitors**
- The GEMA v. Suno ruling (Munich, 31 July 2026) shows EU legal exposure for AI music models (see Q2) — [JUVE Patent](https://juve-patent.com/cases/munich-regional-court-stops-suno-using-gema-protected-music)

### Inferences
- **Threat ranking:**
  1. Free bouquet generators and clones. They set the reference price at €0 and are trivially reproducible: several launched in 2025–2026 and at least one reports $0 MRR.
  2. General AI video and music tools. They let anyone make a "blooming bouquet with a song" clip, though without a dedicated, guided gift flow.
  3. Incumbent e-card subscriptions at about €30/yr, which already include animated flower cards with music.
  4. Florists and e-florists. They could add free digital bouquets to physical orders, or they could become partners or resellers.
- **Defensibility levers** (in rough order of strength):
  - Art direction and quality: hundreds of species against 8 in DigiBouquet, plus botanical accuracy and the "language of flowers".
  - A legally clean, proprietary music catalog, which competitors using Artlist or Epidemic cannot legally replicate in this format.
  - The recipient experience: unwrapping or blooming animation, reply or thank-you, scheduled delivery, reminders.
  - Keepsake and physical upsells: HD video, print of the unique bouquet, a uniqueness certificate.
  - French-language, occasion-specific SEO and brand.
  - B2B or group offers, and florist partnerships.
  - None of these is a hard moat. The procedural generator itself is not defensible.
- **Lessons from comparables:**
  - Cameo: novelty- and pandemic-boosted gifting demand can collapse, so keep fixed costs low.
  - Hallmark's exit and Word Forest's closure: plain e-cards struggle to cover costs, including at large incumbents.
  - Songfinch: emotional depth and perceived human craft support high prices and scale.
  - Kudoboard: a bootstrapped per-item plus B2B model at about $7–20 per board can sustain a small company.
- **Go/no-go framing:**
  - The evidence supports a **low-capital, bootstrapped test** (micro-entreprise, Stripe, a commissioned music catalog, seed-based hosting) timed for a peak such as Saint-Valentin 2027. The evidence does not support a venture-scale plan.
  - Kill or continue criteria could be: free-to-paid conversion ≥ 2–5% (freemium benchmarks), recipient-to-creator conversion, organic share, and the share of revenue from upsells such as music.
  - The two largest risks are near-zero willingness to pay, because free alternatives exist, and music licensing, if the "optional music" feature uses standard library subscriptions.

- DigiBouquet's usage or traffic numbers were not published.
